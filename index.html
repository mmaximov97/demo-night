Seven hundred thousand words I don't remember.

Everything in the demo sits on one thing: a pile of notes my agents can read. Not a smarter model, not a better prompt — notes. Here are the three rules that keep those notes worth reading, and every link from the talk.

This page started as a voice note. → 4 min of talking → transcribed on a server in my flat → a note → this

Three rules

They're numbered because they're a chain, not a menu. Each one is wasted without the one before it.

01

CollectGather more context than feels reasonable

Four things go into the vault. Specs — what I'm building and why, written before the code. Meetings — every call recorded and transcribed. Voice notes — I talk while walking, it lands as text. Decisions — not only what I picked, but what I turned down and why.

That last one is the category a model can never rebuild by reading the repository. Code shows the decision that survived. Notes show the four that didn't, which is usually the question being asked.

  • 875 notes
  • 726,000 words
  • 13 meeting transcripts
  • since 2 Jul 2025
02

CurateContext rots, so schedule the cleaning

Models generate quickly. Let everything they write land in your notes and within a month you own a well-formatted archive of confident nonsense. Once a week I delete what's dead and fact-check what's new — models are good at catching a claim with nothing behind it.

The part that actually holds isn't discipline, it's a file. A hundred lines of conventions in the vault root tell the agent where notes go, how tasks are formatted, and what never to write. Curation is a config, not a mood.

  • weekly pass
  • 101 lines of conventions
  • fact-check by model, decision by me
03

ReachContext you can't reach is context you don't have

A server sits in my apartment with no public address, behind NAT like everyone's. A small rented VPS is the meeting point: the server holds a tunnel to it, my phone holds a tunnel to it, and my phone lands inside my home network. The VPS computes nothing.

After that it's just ssh. The session on the server doesn't die when I shut the laptop — I attach to the same screen from the street, and the agent has been working the whole way.

  • session alive 11 days
  • server up 3 weeks
  • WireGuard, not a SaaS tunnel

collect → nothing to curate
curate → nothing worth reaching for
reach → back to collect, from anywhere

The setup

How the phone gets home

Three hops. The middle one is rented and does no work at all — that's the whole trick.

What it buys

The agent is in the chat

Through an MCP server, my agent reads and writes Telegram. It sits in the working chats with the people I build for. Anya designs my nutrition app and reports what's broken in her own words — no ticket, no repro steps, no screenshot.

I don't forward her message to the model. A complaint isn't a task: somebody has to decide what's actually broken, what to touch and what to leave alone. That stays my job. What the agent receives is the decision, and it reads the chat, the repository and the notes on its own — I never describe the project.

Then it's a push to staging, three and a half minutes of CI, and a rule I don't bend: production waits for Anya to say ок. She's a better QA than my test suite.

On that Telegram server: I run it, I didn't write it. I did hit a wall — it couldn't post media into forum topics — so I added reply_to to send_file and send_album, wrote the tests, and sent it upstream as PR #174. Still open. That's the honest shape of this setup: you end up fixing the tools you depend on.

Open source

Things I built and left public

  • selfhost-ai-lab

    Self-hosted, OpenAI-compatible gateway. Turns one machine into a local provider of LLM chat, speech-to-text and text-to-speech — your projects point at one base URL instead of a vendor. This is what transcribes my meetings.

    TypeScript · runs the transcription in the demo

  • claude-dash-cli

    Terminal dashboard for Claude usage limits, built to live in a tmux pane. Answers how much runway is left and where today's budget went, without leaving the terminal. Zero dependencies.

    JavaScript · MIT · zero deps

  • company-leadership-agent

    Discover and query company leadership through a lean RAG agent. Small on purpose — a readable example of retrieval that doesn't turn into a framework.

    Python

  • jump-frame

    Measure vertical jump height from video in the browser. Step through frames to mark takeoff and landing, get height from flight time. Auto FPS detection and honest error margins.

    TypeScript · runs entirely client-side
